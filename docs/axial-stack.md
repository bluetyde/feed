# The axial stack (plan 12)

A case may describe the pin as **N slices along its height**, each a radial problem with its own heat rate and its own coolant (plan 12, [docs/plans/12-axial-stack-and-plate.md](plans/12-axial-stack-and-plate.md)). **Status 2026-10-14 (milestone A3): a steady stack and a transient on a stack run through the real solver and are read back and checked slice by slice** (the editor's Stack item and the per-slice results are A6, below; the 3D view is A7). **Milestone A5 (2026-10-16): the same section on a plate** (see "The axial conduction of a plate" and [docs/plate.md](plate.md)). Not for mechanics, hot dimensions or burnup, nor with a point-kinetics pulse (refused, with the reason).

```json
"power": { "linearHeatRate": { "value": 20, "unit": "kW/m" } },
"axial": {
  "length": { "value": 0.381, "unit": "m" },
  "slices": 10,
  "powerShape": { "kind": "cosine" },
  "boundary": [
    { "height": { "value": 0, "unit": "m" },     "bulkTemperature": { "value": 573, "unit": "K" }, "heatTransferCoefficient": { "value": 30000, "unit": "W/m2/K" } },
    { "height": { "value": 0.381, "unit": "m" }, "bulkTemperature": { "value": 633, "unit": "K" }, "heatTransferCoefficient": { "value": 30000, "unit": "W/m2/K" } }
  ]
}
```

- **`power.linearHeatRate` is the AVERAGE over the heated length** (what OFFBEAT takes, and what a total power over the length gives); the hottest slice's heat rate is derived and shown. (Plan 12 first said "the peak"; the probe found OFFBEAT's keyword is the average, so the case follows it.)
- **`powerShape`**: `flat`, `cosine` (cos of pi (z/L - 1/2): zero at both ends, peak in the middle) or `table` (`points`: `{position, relativePower}`, positions in length units from the bottom, the first 0 and the last the length, strictly increasing, relative power at least 0 and not all zero, linear between points). FEED normalises the shape so that its integral over the height is 1; the writer will give OFFBEAT exactly that table (it aborts when the integral is not 1 to 1e-4).
- **`boundary`** (optional): a table of `height` against `bulkTemperature` and `heatTransferCoefficient`, or against `surfaceTemperature` for a prescribed wall, in the same form as the case's own `boundary`, linear between points, from height 0 to the length (the heights are in **metres from the bottom**, as OFFBEAT's table is). Without it every slice has the case's own boundary. The film coefficient is 0.001 to 1e9 W/m2/K as in a boundary history.
- **Slices**: 3 to 50. Each slice's heat rate is the average times the shape **at the slice's centre** (the way OFFBEAT samples a profile), its coolant the table at the same height.
- Every refusal has the code `E_AXIAL` (or `E_UNITS`, `E_UNSUPPORTED`) and names the field; 34 vectors (`schema/vectors/good/axial-*`, `bad/axial-*`).

## Warnings

- **`W_AXIAL_SHAPE_STEP`**: two neighbouring slices differ in heat rate by more than 40 % of the largest slice's, so the shape is not resolved by this many slices. A cosine needs about 8 slices to stay under it.
- **`W_AXIAL_CONDUCTION`**: the estimate below is above 2 %. The warning for a short pin or a steep shape.
- **`W_POWER_RANGE`** is judged on the **hottest slice**, not the average.

## The axial conduction

OFFBEAT solves the stack as one mesh with N layers per solid, so the layers exchange a little heat along the pin (the probe, [docs/spike/axial-plate-probe.md](spike/axial-plate-probe.md)). FEED's independent solutions are one-dimensional per slice and do not. The size is estimated (`axialConductionEstimate` in `src/core/axial.ts`) from a fin model: lambda squared = (k A of fuel and cladding at their largest conductivity) / (the conductance per length from the pin to the coolant), times the largest second difference of the shape across the slices (1/m2) relative to its largest value; for the cosine this is lambda^2 (pi / L)^2.

**Calibration:** against the real-solver measurements at L = 0.19, 0.381 and 0.762 m (10 layers, cosine, 20 kW/m average), the estimate times the peak rise covers the measured end-layer change (1.06 K at 0.381 m) and peak-layer change (0.35 K) and is within a factor 2 of the end-layer change; it goes as 1/L^2 as the measurements do (`tests/axialCase.test.ts`).

**Limits:** measured for the cosine only; an adiabatic end with a steep shape (a table with a large end slope) makes a boundary layer that the estimate does not include, so A2 measures such a shape before the estimate is used as a tolerance there; interior slices only. The estimate is an alarm now, and from A2 a part of the cross-check tolerance (with a factor 3).

## The editor

The server's check of a stack returns the one-dimensional closed form of every slice (`axial.slices`: height, heat rate, coolant, centre, fuel-surface and outer-wall temperatures, the property-range verdict), the hottest slice, and the conduction estimate; the quick look and the radial profile are those of the hottest slice. The editor's panel and the 3D view are milestones A6 and A7.

## How it is written and checked (A2)

- **The mesh.** One OFFBEAT run: each solid has N layers over the heated length (`blockMeshDict`: `fuel (nf 1 N)`, `cladding (nc 1 N)`, the height in mm), the end faces stay `empty`. `lhgr` is the average heat rate and `heatSourceOptions.axialProfile` is `constantTabulated` with the shape as a table normalised to an integral of 1 over 0 to 1 (OFFBEAT aborts otherwise; the cosine is written with 41 points). With a coolant table the outer wall is `timeDependentAxialProfileHTC` (or `timeDependentAxialProfileT` for a prescribed wall): heights in metres, two time points `0` and twice the end time (a table must reach past the end of the run), the same row at both. Without one the case's own boundary is written on every layer. A case without a stack is written exactly as before.
- **Reading it back.** The cells come block by block (all fuel cells layer by layer, then all cladding cells); the parser takes layer k's fuel cells `k nf .. (k+1) nf - 1` and cladding cells `N nf + k nc ..` and **refuses output whose cell heights do not fit the layers** (a reordered mesh would otherwise be read as a wrong but plausible result). The outer faces come from the `cladOuter` patch's value list, one per layer.
- **The checks, per slice.** Each slice is judged as the one-radial-slice pin it is: its own heat rate and coolant, FEED's closed form for the fuel and the cladding, the wall temperature, the flux through the cladding (the energy balance), the property ranges, the temperature limits, the steady-state gate. The result reports **one line per check**: a failing slice if there is one (with how many fail and where), otherwise the worst value (the smallest margin for a temperature limit). The tolerances are the one-dimensional ones **plus 3 times the estimated axial conduction** (`allowance` in the record): that many K on a field, W/m on a flux and K on the wall (the factor 3 is a margin over the estimate, which covered the measurements within a factor 2).
- **The whole pin.** `energyBalanceTotal`: the heat the cladding walls carry away, summed over the slices, against the heat given (the shape sampled at the slice centres, which is what OFFBEAT deposits and can differ from the stated average: 0.46 % for 10 slices of a cosine). The end faces carry no heat, so this balance is exact whatever the axial conduction does; it passes to 3e-6 on the real runs. `axialConduction` is an informational line (verdict `na`) that states the estimate and the widening.
- **The record.** A stack's results are the hottest slice's (`summary`, `radial`, so every existing view, export and comparison still works), plus `axial`: the heated length, the hottest slice, one entry per slice (height, heat rate, coolant, the temperatures that the editor shows, the radial profile), the sampled and stated average heat rates, the conduction estimate and the allowance. The banner adds that this is one radial solution per slice, with the heat flow along the pin that the solver includes.
- **A limit with a cladding condition** (the TRIGA fuel limit applies 1150 C while the cladding is at most 500 C and 950 C above it) is judged **per slice, with that slice's cladding**: a slice's own cladding temperature decides its limit.
- **Shown to fail.** Real output judged against a stack that was not run fails: a shape read as a ramp or its mirror, a coolant table read from the top, 10 % more heat (the closed-form checks and the whole-pin balance). `tests/axialSolver.test.ts` (real solver), `tests/stackResults.test.ts` (saved real output in `tests/golden/offbeat-stack/`).

## A transient on a stack (A3)

- **What the case says.** `transient` as for a single slice, with `axial` beside it. **The power history is the AVERAGE over the heated length** (as `power.linearHeatRate` is for a steady case); each slice's own history is that times the slice's factor (the shape at its centre relative to the average). `initialSteadyLinearHeatRate` is the average too: every slice starts at **its own** one-dimensional steady state at its own heat rate. A `boundaryHistory` applies to every slice equally; an `axial.boundary` table (the coolant along the height) holds for the whole run. **The two together are refused** (the coolant would be a function of both height and time), and so is a point-kinetics pulse (it is generated from one fuel temperature). The work budget (cells x steps) counts all the slices.
- **The files.** As for the steady stack: N layers per solid, the power as `timePoints` and `lhgr` (the average history) times the normalised axial table, the history with axial positions `(0 L)` in metres, the starting field written for every cell (every fuel layer, then every cladding layer).
- **The checks.** `buildStackTransientResults` cuts the output into slices and gives each, as text of its own, to the one-slice transient builder (plan 08, plan 11: cross-check against the independent finite-volume solution, energy balance, time step, wall, property ranges, limits). The independent solutions are N sets of the three runs each, in the worker thread. One line per check as for the steady stack (a failing slice, else the worst), plus `energyBalanceTotal` (the slices' energies summed: the end faces carry no heat, so the axial flow cancels and no allowance is made) and the informational `axialConduction`. The record is the hottest slice's, plus `axial` with one series (power, peak, wall) per slice.
- **The allowance.** The cross-check of a slice is widened by **3 times the steady estimate as a fraction of the largest rise of any slice** (K), and its energy balance by 3 times the estimate as a fraction of the slice's own energy scaled by the ratio of the largest factor to its own (the end slices make little heat). The rise of a slice is the independent solution's peak over the whole run above its coolant, not the steady rise at the history's peak (a pulse's peak is 10 MW/m).
- **What the real runs showed** (`tests/axialTransientSolver.test.ts`, golden output in `tests/golden/offbeat-stack-transient/`): a 10 ms pulse on an operating cosine stack, a pump coastdown on a cosine stack, and an asymmetric shape all pass every check (worst deviation from the independent solution 1.2, 3.0 and 1.0 K against bounds of 13, 9 and 7 K); five equal slices equal the one-slice pin to 1e-3 K; judged against a stack that was not run (the shape read from the top, 20 % more power, half the film coefficient) the checks fail by tens to hundreds of kelvin.
- **A stack that starts from a steady state does not start in the solver's own steady state.** Each slice starts at its own one-dimensional steady state, and the solver's coupled (r, z) steady state differs from that by the axial conduction (1 K at 0.381 m, 45 K at 0.06 m, from the steady runs), so the run relaxes toward it. On a short stack (0.06 m, 12 slices) over a 60 s coastdown the end slice differs from the independent solution by 31 K at 5 s, a quarter of what the allowance gives (136 K); the allowance is what that check rests on (`short stack` tests).
- **A limit, stated.** The allowance is the steady estimate, which is not a bound for a transient, and on a short stack it is loose where the transient is short: a 0.1 s pulse on the 0.06 m stack differs by 1.6 K and is allowed 194 K (12 % of its largest rise, about 1600 K), so that check only catches gross errors there. The whole-pin energy balance has no allowance and is unaffected. A time-dependent bound (the heat has had only so long to flow) is a possible later tightening. The validator's warning `W_AXIAL_CONDUCTION` is the signal that a stack is short enough for this to matter. (A5 adds a bound for the heat the slices exchange during a run, below.)

> **Since plan 15 (B2 to B7), a stack of plates is judged against a two-dimensional reference by default** (the last section of this page). The sections on the axial conduction of a plate and on the heat the slices exchange describe the one-dimensional regime with the allowance and the exchange bound: it is still what a pin uses, what a plate falls back to when the reference cannot be computed, and the contrast shown beside the reference.

## The axial conduction of a plate (A5)

**What applies.** The fin model of `axialConductionEstimate` carries over unchanged in form: the solids carry heat along the height with the conductance k A and lose it through the thickness to the coolant with the conductance per unit height U, so the correction to a slice is about lambda^2 T'' with lambda^2 = k A / U. What changes is A: a plate's cross-sections along the height are strips of one metre width (the meat's half-thickness, the cladding's thickness), taken from the geometry kernel as the cell volume per unit height (`geometryOf(c.plate).volume`), which for a pin is the annulus pi (R^2 - r^2) it always was. U is again the hottest slice's heat rate over its centre-to-coolant rise (per metre of width), so the estimate stays conservative: the centre is hotter than the section's mean. For the synthetic plate lambda^2 = 1.96e-5 m2, the same order as the synthetic pin's (2e-5): a plate is thick in the direction that carries the heat along the height (4.1 mm of meat against 4.1 mm of radius) and the flux per unit of cross-section is lower.

**Calibration on the real solver** (cosine, 10 slices, 200 kW/m on average, three lengths, `tests/plateStackSolver.test.ts`; the saved runs are re-read by `tests/plateStackCase.test.ts`):

| Length | Peak rise | Estimate x rise | Peak slice measured | End slice measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.19 m | 295.6 K | 1.60 K | -0.80 K | +2.39 K |
| 0.381 m | 296.2 K | 0.397 K | -0.199 K | +0.609 K |
| 0.762 m | 296.3 K | 0.099 K | -0.050 K | +0.153 K |

Every halving of the length multiplies the change by 4.0 (1/L^2, as for the pin). The estimate times the rise is 2.0 times the peak slice's change and 0.65 to 0.67 times the end slice's, at every length: the end slice is warmed by the step in the sampled shape between it and its neighbour (a one-sided difference, about three times the interior second difference of a cosine; the estimate uses interior slices only, see A1), which is why the checks widen their tolerance by **3 times the estimate** (the end slice's change is 1.5 times the estimate, so the margin there is 2). The pin's calibration (end within a factor 2 of the estimate) is the same. The estimate is an alarm (`W_AXIAL_CONDUCTION` above 2 %) and, in the results, 3 times it widens the per-slice tolerances.

**Limits, measured.** (1) A shape with steep ends: a table that goes from 0.3 to 1.0 of the maximum within a tenth of the length, 20 slices over 0.381 m, warms the end slice by 1.9 K and cools the next two by 0.93 and 0.95 K against an allowance of 6.0 K (3.1 times the largest change; `tests/plateStackSolver.test.ts`, golden `steep`); the same shape on 10 slices and on a 0.12 m plate (probes, not kept as tests) gave 0.49 K against 2.9 K and 4.4 K against 30 K. (2) A pure ramp has no curvature, so the estimate is zero and the allowance nothing; the end slice is still warmed (0.16 K on a 24 K step between slices) and the mesh bound (about 1 K) covers it, which is a coincidence of size, not a guarantee: a steeper ramp on a shorter plate is not covered, and `W_AXIAL_SHAPE_STEP` (a step of more than 40 % of the maximum between neighbours) is the warning. (3) The estimate is for the steady stack; a transient has its own addition, below.

## The heat the slices exchange during a transient (A5)

**Found by the plate's loss of cooling.** The A3 allowance for a transient is the steady estimate as a fraction of the largest rise. The first real run of the F17 case on a cosine plate stack (100 kW/m on average, the film coefficient falling to 100 W/m2/K, 60 s) **failed** the cross-check and the energy balance of the end slice: 5.6 K against 2.6 K allowed, 7.3 % against 5.5 %. When the coolant stops carrying the heat away, the heat made in the middle of the plate has only the way along the height to reach the end slices, and the steady estimate (which assumes the coolant wins) does not bound that. A3 had named this: "the allowance is the steady estimate, which is not a bound for a transient".

**The bound** (`axialExchange` in `src/core/axial.ts`, `exchangeAllowances` in `src/core/stackTransientResults.ts`). A slice gains heat (k A / h^2)(T_{k+1} - 2 T_k + T_{k-1}) per unit height and time from its neighbours (h the slice height; an end slice has one neighbour). Over a run its temperature differs from the one-dimensional solution by at most (k A / (C h^2)) times the integral of the absolute second difference of the slices' independent solutions (their hottest cell is the temperature, from the trace the independent solution already keeps); the loss to the coolant, which damps it, is left out, so this is a first-order bound, not an estimate. k is the largest conductivity and C (the heat capacity of the two solids per unit height) the smallest over the temperatures the run reaches. As energy it is the same quantity over the energy the slice is given. Each slice takes the larger of this bound (times 2) and the steady allowance. Both the cross-check and the slice energy balance use it. The same code serves pins and plates.

**Where it holds, measured on the real solver** (the "exchange number" is the rate k A / (C h^2) times the run time):

| Run | Exchange number | Measured change | Bound at factor 1 | Allowed (factor 2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plate 0.381 m, 10 slices, film to 100 W/m2/K, 60 s | 0.06 | 5.6 K | 7.1 K | 14.3 K |
| Plate 0.381 m, film to 10 W/m2/K, 200 s | 0.21 | 64.4 K | 72.8 K | 145.7 K |
| Plate 0.1 m, 10 slices of 10 mm, film to 100 W/m2/K, 60 s | 0.91 | 61.7 K | 103.3 K | 206.6 K |
| Plate 0.2 m, 20 slices of 10 mm, film to 100 W/m2/K, 100 s | 1.52 | 64.9 K (end), 16.2 K (next) | | not counted: fails |

The bound was 1.1 to 1.7 times the measured change in the three runs where it holds, hence the factor 2 (a margin over a first-order bound whose temperature is the slice's hottest cell). **Beyond an exchange number of 1 the bound is not counted** (`AXIAL_EXCHANGE_NUMBER_LIMIT`): the heat then spreads over several slices, the neighbour of the end slice differs from its one-dimensional solution by more than its own first-order bound (16.2 K against 9.4 K at factor 2: the end slice, 65 K, has moved and the neighbour feels it), and the check fails; the note of the `axialConduction` line says "ABOVE 1 ... NOT counted" and why. The failure is the right answer: the one-dimensional solutions are no longer a reference for a run in which heat crosses several slices. A longer plate, fewer slices (a larger h) or a shorter run keeps the number small; a plate of 0.6 m in 10 slices of 60 mm has a number of 0.03 for 60 s.

**What it did to the pin.** The pin's checks are unchanged where the number is above 1 (the 60 s coastdown on the 0.06 m stack: number 4.8, allowance still 136 K), and loosened where it is below 1 and the bound is larger than the steady allowance (the pin's 60 s coastdown on 0.381 m: the cross-check bound goes from 3.4 K to 10.7 K for a measured 3.0 K, because the bound does not know the coolant is working). The negative tests (shape from the top, 20 % more power, half the film coefficient) still fail by tens to hundreds of kelvin on both geometries.

## In the editor (milestone A6)

- **Stack item** (explorer, under Pin): *One slice (the hottest)* or *A stack of slices*. A stack has a heated length, 3 to 50 slices, a power shape (flat, cosine, or a table of points with the position from the bottom and the relative power; FEED scales it to an average of 1) and, optionally, a **coolant table against height** (heights from the bottom; bulk temperature and film coefficient, or a prescribed wall, in the form of the coolant boundary: changing that form removes the table, with a note in the log). The Power item then reads **q′ average** over the heated length.
- **What the case gives.** Under the inputs, the server's check is shown: the average and the largest slice's heat rate, the hottest slice and its centre temperature (each slice's one-dimensional closed form, no heat flow between slices), the estimate of what the heat flow along the pin could add, a two-panel plot against height (heat rate; centre, fuel surface, outer wall and the coolant) and a table of the slices with the hottest marked. The banner above the plots says that this is one radial solution per slice. The plots have the same data as a text table for a screen reader.
- **Results.** A run of a stack shows a *Hottest slice* card and, under the profile, a plot of the peak temperature of every slice against height (OFFBEAT's hottest cell and centre cell, and the closed form of each slice as a dashed line; for a transient the peak over the run and each slice's factor) with a table (slice, height, heat rate or factor, hottest cell, centre cell or the time of the peak). The profile, cards, checks and exports above it are the hottest slice's, as before. A case without a stack looks and works as it did.
- **Not here.** The 3D view of the stack is A7. A stack of plates (milestone A5) is accepted by the validator and shown the same way; the page draws its plate as a slab, and its results view is the pin's.

## The 3D view (milestone A7)

A **3D stack** tab beside *Pin and profile* (case view, steady stacks) and a panel under the per-slice plot (results view, steady runs) draw the stack as a solid you can turn: every slice's rings coloured by temperature, a quarter cut away so that the two cut faces show the radial profile along the height, the cladding's outer wall on the outside. **It is a rendering of N one-dimensional results and says so:** the label *1.5D: one radial solution per slice* is drawn into the picture (in a run: *1.5D: OFFBEAT's result in each slice*) and a caption under it says that it is not a three-dimensional solution and that nothing varies around the axis. In the case view the caption adds that its closed forms let no heat flow between the slices; in a run it says that OFFBEAT's result does include that heat, and prints FEED's estimate of its size (the check `axialConduction`, as a percentage of the largest rise). Neither can be switched off. The radius is drawn larger than the true radius against the height (the factor is printed on the picture); a plate is drawn as a slab cut at its middle plane, its width a drawing width (the model is per metre of width).
- **The numbers.** In the case view the temperatures are each slice's one-dimensional closed form (the server returns the radial profile of every slice, `axialProfiles`, 17 points in the fuel and 17 in the cladding; the same numbers as the per-slice summary); in the results view they are OFFBEAT's cell values of each slice. The colour scale is the pin picture's (`heat()`), over the stack's lowest and highest ring temperature, in the unit the page shows. **A table of the same numbers** (slice, height, innermost ring, hottest ring, outer wall) sits under the picture in a section that opens with the keyboard; it is built from the same rings as the drawing, and a test compares them.
- **Turning.** Drag, or focus the picture and use the arrow keys (turn and tilt), plus and minus (zoom), `c` (cut-away on and off), `0` (reset); buttons do the same. Under the pointer, a readout names the slice, its height, the radius and the temperature.
- **The option for plates (plan 15, B5).** For a steady stack of plates the case view can draw FEED's own two-dimensional field, with the heat flow along the height in it, instead of the closed forms (last section of this page); the caption says which is drawn.
- **Not here.** A transient stack has no 3D view (the peak of each slice over the run is in the plot above it); a particle's sphere is drawn by milestone T5 of plan 13.

## Examples (milestone A9)

`examples/ksu-triga-cosine-stack.json` is the K-State TRIGA hot-element case (with the SAR's film coefficient and fuel temperature limits) as a stack of 10 slices over 0.381 m. The length is the UT-Austin SAR's fuel length (Table 4.1; the K-State text FEED holds states none), the cosine shape and the 5 K warming of the pool (20 C at the bottom, 25 C at the top) are assumptions, and the average heat rate (39.48 kW/m) is chosen so that the cosine's continuous peak is the SAR's 62.021 kW/m. On the real solver every check passes: the hottest slice is the sixth (centre 776.81 K, 503.7 C; the fifth, which makes the same heat in a pool 0.5 K colder, is 776.31 K), 646 K under the 1150 C limit; the closed form of the one-dimensional slice is 781.19 K, so the heat flow along the element lowers the peak by 4.4 K and warms the end slices by 10.9 K (381.2 K against 370.3 K). The conduction estimate is 1.05 % of the largest rise and the per-slice tolerance is widened by 15.3 K on a field, so the end slice passes its closed-form check at 10.9 K against 15.8 K allowed, and its energy balance at 12.9 % because the end slice makes little heat and the flux the axial flow adds (1.26 kW/m) is large against it (1.93 kW/m allowed); the whole-element balance holds to 2.5e-6. The case shows what the stack adds over the single hot slice, and that for a high-conductivity element the end slices are where the one-dimensional picture is weakest.

## The two-dimensional reference for a stack of plates (plan 15, B1 to B7)

**What changed.** A stack of **plates** is no longer judged against N separate one-dimensional solutions with a widened tolerance. It is judged against FEED's own **two-dimensional finite-volume solution of the whole stack** (`src/core/axialFv.ts`, cut back into slices by `src/core/stackReference.ts`): the same layers and the same through-thickness cells as the writer's mesh, joined along the height by the conduction between layers of the same solid, the end faces insulated, the gap a conductance, each layer with its own heat rate (the shape at the layer's centre, as OFFBEAT samples it) and its own coolant. The heat that flows along the height is in the reference, so the checks use the **mesh bound alone and no allowance**. A pin is **not** changed: its record is character for character the one it was before plan 15 (the pin's radial offset of about 3.4 K between FEED's kernel and OFFBEAT's mesh is larger than the 1 K it would have to resolve; the owner decides later whether a short pin should use the reference).

**What the reference is.** A second method on the same discretisation, written without OFFBEAT's code: radial cells and conductances as the one-dimensional finite-volume solution (`transientFv.ts`, through the geometry kernel), the conductivity between two layers linearly interpolated to their common face (the arithmetic mean of the two, the layers having one height; the harmonic mean, two half cells in series, was tried first and leaves 0.2 K against OFFBEAT's runs with a conductivity that depends on temperature, see the plan 15 record), a banded direct solve, Picard iteration for conductivity and heat capacity that depend on temperature (enthalpy form, so stored energy is exact), implicit Euler for a transient. It agrees with the saved OFFBEAT plate runs to the digits OFFBEAT prints (5e-6 K steady; at most 0.0094 K in the transient runs, which reach 3000 K).

**What it is not.** Not a measurement and not an independent physical model: it shares the mesh and the scheme with the solver (the plate's discretisation is the simplest one, so there is little else to differ). It cannot find a fault in the discretisation, the property laws or the mesh that the solver and the reference share. The one-dimensional closed forms (the quick look, the per-slice table, the editor) are unchanged. It was compared with OFFBEAT only for constant conductivities and heat capacities (all the saved stack runs have them); the face conductivity between layers when the conductivity depends on temperature is untested against the solver (the two choices differ by 0.0002 K on a plate in the probe's made-up law).

**The steady checks.** Fuel and cladding cells against the reference (the bound is 5 dT / N^2 as before, plus a floor of 0.01 K for the 8 digits OFFBEAT writes), the outer wall against the reference's wall (0.05 K), the flux through the two innermost cladding cells against the reference's flux through the same two cells (1 %: a layer's wall flux is its own heat plus what its neighbours give it along the height), and the whole plate's balance from the cells' own fluxes. The check ids are unchanged; the labels say "two-dimensional reference", and each note gives, for contrast, the deviation of the one-dimensional closed form from the same cells. The record has `axial.reference` (`kind: "two-dimensional"`, the effect of the heat flow along the height in the cells, the one-dimensional solutions' deviation and the reference's deviation) and keeps `axial.allowance` as the estimate the old way would have added (information only). The `axialConduction` line says so.

**The transient checks.** The reference is the stack's transient solution from the field the run started from, with the run's step, powers and coolants. A slice's energy balance counts the heat it receives from its neighbours (deposited + received = stored + rejected), taken from the reference. **There is no exchange bound and no exchange-number limit**: the 0.2 m, 20-slice, 100 s loss of cooling that failed the cross-check and the slice energy balance (exchange number 1.5) passes every check. The spatial and time-stepping error estimates in each slice's bound are those of its own one-dimensional runs (the doubled mesh and the half step), carried over as differences, because the radial mesh and the Euler step do not depend on the axial term to first order.

**The start.** A transient that starts from a steady state starts, in the run, with **each slice at its own one-dimensional steady state** (the field the writer gives OFFBEAT), not at the stack's coupled steady state; the two differ by the steady axial effect (0.304 K for the 0.381 m loss of cooling at 100 kW/m, 4 K for the 0.1 m plate). The reference starts from the same field as the run, so that it follows the run; the record states the distance (`startDistance`). Starting the run itself from the coupled state (`coupledSteadyStart`, tested as a fixed point of the transient) would change what OFFBEAT is given, and is not done here.

**When the reference cannot be computed.** Three limits, each with its reason in the record: more than 20000 unknowns (layers times cells per layer; steady and transient); for a transient, more than 3e6 unknowns times steps (the validator's own budget) or more than **4e8 of banded-solve work** (unknowns times cells per layer times steps: the solve costs rows x n^2 per step, so 10 layers of 100 cells and 3000 steps are 3e6 unknown-steps and 3e8 of work and took 40 s, while 50 layers of 26 cells and 2300 steps are 3e6 and 8e7 and took 3.5 s); and a **deadline of 60 s** of wall-clock time. Or an iteration that does not settle. The record then falls back to the one-dimensional solutions with the allowance and the exchange bound of plan 12 and says why in `axial.reference` (`kind: "allowance"`, `reason`); the run is not failed for it.

**Where it runs (B4).** A transient's reference is solved in a **worker thread of its own, with its own deadline**, beside the thread that does the one-dimensional runs of the slices (`src/core/independentRunner.ts`, job kind `stackReference`; the pattern and the cancel and in-process behaviour of [independent-runs.md](independent-runs.md)). The run manager waits for both; a cancel stops both; a reference that passes its deadline, is over a cap, or fails in the solver does **not** fail the run (the one-dimensional checks are a complete verification with the allowance): the checks keep the allowance, the record's `axial.reference.reason` and the run's notes give the reason, and the notes say where the reference ran and how long it took. Without the compiled worker the reference runs in the server's thread and the solver checks the clock itself between steps, so its deadline still applies. The worker's solution and the record built from it equal the in-thread ones exactly (tested). Measured on this machine (shared, 40 to 60 % load from other work): 10 slices, 26 cells, 1200 steps 0.33 s; 50 slices, 26 cells, 1200 steps 1.6 s; 50 slices, 26 cells, 2300 steps (the largest the validator allows) 3.5 s with constant properties and 13.5 s with a linear k(T) and c(T); 10 layers of 100 cells and 3000 steps 40 s constant, and over the 60 s deadline with k(T) (stopped, with the reason); 4 layers of 250 cells and 3000 steps are refused by the work cap before any solving. The error estimates of a slice's bound come from the cheap one-dimensional runs of the slice, so a doubled two-dimensional mesh (95 to 155 s on the largest case in the probe) is never run.

**The editor's option (B5).** For a **steady stack of plates** the case view's 3D tab has the option *Heat flow along the height: not included (closed form per slice, instant) | included (two-dimensional reference solution)*. Included draws FEED's own steady two-dimensional field of the whole stack (one point per cell, the end faces insulated) through the same picture and the same table, so the drawing and the table say the same numbers. The caption under the picture says which is drawn and cannot be hidden: the closed-form state says the closed forms let no heat flow between the slices; the two-dimensional state says the heat along the height **is** in the temperatures, by how much it changes them (the field against the same solution with the conduction between layers off: 11 K on the 0.06 m, 12-slice plate of the editor check, 0.609 K on the 0.381 m cosine plate at 200 kW/m), that it is FEED's own second method on the mesh of the run and not OFFBEAT's result, and how to get the closed forms back. A run's picture (the results tab) is OFFBEAT's own cells, includes the flow, and has no option. For a **stack of pins** the option is shown disabled with the reason (the pin keeps the closed forms and the allowance); a transient stack has no 3D view. The check response carries `axialField2d` (`available`, the slices' points, `effect`, `note`; or `available: false` and `reason`).

**The oracle entries (B6).** What the tolerances of the SEED entries for plate stacks would be with this reference is measured in the [plan 15 record](plans/15-axial-2d.md) (B6) by `scripts/measure-oracle-2d.cjs`. **No entry was changed.**

**Shown to fail** (against the saved OFFBEAT plate output): the axial term removed (six of the nine steady runs, where the effect is above the mesh bound: the cosine at 0.19 and 0.381 m, the warming coolant, the short plate and the steep-ended table; in the other three it is 0.4 K or less and is not claimed; and in all four loss-of-cooling runs), a shape shifted by one layer, a coolant read from the top, more heat, and a different film coefficient. The numbers are in the [plan 15 record](plans/15-axial-2d.md).

## Pins on the two-dimensional solution where the heat along the height is large (plan 19 D1, 2026-10-24)

A stack of **pins** keeps the widened bound (the allowance), **unless** the heat flow along the height, estimated as for the allowance, is larger than the pin's radial mesh bound (`pinReferenceDecision` in `src/core/stackReference.ts`: the fin-model estimate times the hottest slice's rise, against the fuel's `5 dT / N^2` of that slice). The saved pin runs: 0.381 m, estimate 1.1 K against a bound of 10.3 K, the allowance is kept; 0.06 m, 45 K against 10.3 K, the reference is used (a transient pulse on the 0.06 m pin: 22 600 against 5 160, used).

**How a pin is judged then.** The pin's radial scheme in the two-dimensional solution has its nodes at the middle of each cell, OFFBEAT's cells sit at their volume centroids, and the two differ by 3 to 4 K in a pin (plan 15, B0), so the pin's cells are not compared with the solution cell by cell as a plate's are. They are compared with **the one-dimensional closed form at the cell's own radius plus the effect of the heat along the height**, the effect being the two-dimensional solution minus the same solution with no conduction between the layers (the radial offset is the same in both and cancels). The bound is the mesh bound and a floor of 0.01 K, **no allowance**. A transient pin uses the stack's transient two-dimensional solution as a plate does (the cross-check compares cells index by index, as it always has for a pin). If the second solution cannot be computed (the work cap), the pin keeps the allowance.

**What it did (the saved short pin, 0.06 m, 12 slices).** Steady: OFFBEAT's fuel cells are 0.64 K from "closed form plus effect" (the allowance version passed a 44.7 K deviation on a 135.8 K allowance); the one-dimensional closed form without the effect fails the same check (44.7 K against a bound of 10.3 K), so the check has teeth. Transient (60 s of lost cooling): the cross-check bound falls from the allowance's about 136 K to 5.75 K, the value from 30.7 K to 2.72 K; a cell 20 K off at the end passes under the allowance and fails against the reference. The 0.381 m pins are unchanged, byte for byte against the records saved before plan 15.

**What it does not do.** The case view's steady field (plan 15, B5) is still not offered for a pin (its reason text is unchanged: for an ordinary pin the radial offset hides the 1 K). A pin with the reference has a `reference` record in `axial` like a plate, with the same fields.
