# M0 of plan 17: what OFFBEAT's mechanics does through a TRIGA pulse

Done 2026-10-20 for [plan 17](../plans/17-transient-mechanics.md), milestone M0. **Question:** does the pinned OFFBEAT (`80e84450`, OpenFOAM 9) run FEED's TRIGA pulse with the mechanics on and a contact between the fuel and the cladding, and does the independent thermo-elastic solution agree with it at the times of the run, when the gap closes and when it opens again? Nothing under `src/`, `schema/`, `tests/` or `library/` was changed. The probe composes the OFFBEAT case by editing what FEED's writer wrote (FEED's validator refuses `mechanics` with a `transient`, on purpose), runs it in the `feed-solver` distro, and reads the fields of every written time. Scripts, saved outputs and the tables: [transient-mechanics-probe/](transient-mechanics-probe/) (`runs.sh` lists every run with its exact arguments).

## Verdict on the gate

**The gate is met for the contact, with FEED's writer changed in two places. With the writer as it is, the gate fails.** In more detail:

- **FEED's writer as it is does not work on this pulse.** Its explicit penalty contact (`gapContact`, factor 0.7 or 0.3) does not converge in the displacement once the solids touch: every step from the first contact on runs to the cap of 1000 outer iterations (475 of 900 steps up to 45 ms; 498 of 502 steps with a zero cold gap) and OFFBEAT accepts and writes such steps silently. The contact pressure is then wrong by 4 % to 260 % and the gap width has the wrong sign. At the one factor that does converge (0.1) the pressure is 1.8 % low but the cladding's displacement misses its 1 % bound at 111 of 117 times with contact. Also, the central hole of the fuel has the wrong displacement condition (`zeroGradient`), which gives a stress of 19 MPa where a uniformly heated ring has none.
- **With the implicit contact (`implicitGapContact`, OFFBEAT's own, named in the case file) and a traction-free hole, every step of every run converges** (2000 pulse steps, then 1998 cooldown steps of 50 ms after a restart), and the contact pressure follows the independent solution through the closing, the peak and the reopening for the factors 0.3, 0.5 and 0.7, for cold gaps of 0, 20 and 40 micron: the error is 0.25 to 0.7 % (-0.26 % at the peak at 0.7, -0.62 % at 0.3, 20 micron), the gap after reopening agrees to 0.01 micron, and the displacement, stress and contact checks pass at **every** written time with contact, 117 of 117 for 20 micron, 103 of 103 for 40 micron, 200 of 200 for the zero gap. This is the soft U-ZrH against steel that the steady sweep had at the edge of its tolerance: with the implicit contact the factor 0.7 is stable and 0.3 to 0.7 all give less than 1 % in the pressure (D7).
- **What does not meet the steady tolerances, said plainly (nothing was widened):** (a) the radial-balance check of OFFBEAT's own stress fails at 5 times for 20 micron (18 to 22 ms) and 8 times for 40 micron (18 to 25 ms), around the start of contact, by up to 2.0 times its bound, while a steep thermal front crosses the 8 cladding cells (it fails at one time with contact, the first one after closing); the zero-gap run has no failure; (b) the stress check fails at 6 early times (4 to 9 ms, 4 to 12 ms for 40 micron) where every stress is below 0.35 MPa and the bound's floor of 10 kPa is under the solver's noise (it printed 20 to 170 kPa there); (c) with FEED's finite-volume temperatures instead of OFFBEAT's own, the pulse (0 to 100 ms) passes at every time with contact, but the cooldown fails the displacement at 12 and the contact at 3 of 117 times with contact, because the two temperature fields differ by up to 1.75 K, which the plan already says needs an allowance (below); (d) the thermal strain has to be piecewise constant per cell in the independent solution, as the finite volumes are: interpolating the cell temperatures linearly instead gives 22 % to 38 % error in the contact pressure and 14 to 19 failed contact checks with contact (below).
- The decision for the owner is therefore not "stop": the plan can go on, but **D7 changes (implicit contact), D3 changes (OFFBEAT takes the laws itself, option (b)), the writer has four changes, and M3's yardstick needs three stated additions** (the sections "What it changes").

## What was run

The example [`ut-triga-pulse-4p40-limits.json`](../../examples/ut-triga-pulse-4p40-limits.json): 100 ms in steps of 0.05 ms (2000), the power history as given (peak 237.6 MW/m at 30 ms), 60 + 8 cells, the fuel with its 3.175 mm hole, gap conductance 4096 W/m2/K, film 10 kW/m2/K, the start at 20 degC. Built by FEED's writer (`dist/core`) twice and merged by text edits: the pulse as a thermal-only transient, and a steady mechanics sibling with the same geometry and materials (for `0/D`, `fvSolution`, `rheologyOptions`, `mechanicsSolverOptions`); `mechanicsSolver smallStrain`; the reference temperature 293.15 K; the fuel and steel as FEED reduces them to constants (**the fuel's E 35.59 GPa and alpha 1.2609e-5 per K, FEED's rule applied to the finite-volume profile at 100 ms**, outside the library's modulus range of 293 to 923 K on purpose: this probe tests the machinery, D3 is question 5; the steel 190 GPa, 17.2e-6 per K; Poisson 0.32 and 0.3).

- **Cold gaps.** 0 (the example), 20 and 40 micron. For 20 and 40 micron **the gap is cut from the cladding wall** (the inner radius grows, the outer stays at 18.669 mm: walls of 0.488 and 0.468 mm instead of 0.508); a run with the wall kept at 0.508 mm is used only for the cost without contact.
- **A cooldown after the pulse** so that the gap reopens: the power table of the pulse followed by a ramp to zero 0.5 ms after 100 ms; a restart (`startFrom latestTime`) with steps of 50 ms to 100 s. The film coefficient and bulk temperature are the example's. 100 outputs in each stage (1 ms, then 1 s): 200 written times.
- **Penalty factors 0.3, 0.5, 0.7**, in the implicit and the explicit form.
- **The independent solution** is `thermoElastic.ts` (`solveSlice`, built `dist/core`) with the same constants and a temperature field of the run's written time, as a step function over the cells (the finite-volume temperature of each cell for the whole cell), evaluated (a) with OFFBEAT's own cell temperatures and (b) with the temperatures of FEED's independent finite-volume run (`transientFv.ts`, same power table, same steps and restart). The yardstick is the steady plan's tolerances unchanged: displacement 1 % of the solid's largest + 1e-9 m, stress 3 % of the stress scale + 10 kPa, equilibrium 2 % + 10 kPa, contact pressure 3 % + 10 kPa, gap 2 % + 1e-8 m (the code of the checks was re-written in `analyze.cjs`, not imported, because `mechanicsChecks.ts` takes the closed-form temperatures of a steady case).
- **Pass/fail of "touching"**: OFFBEAT writes `interfaceP` values of 1e-6 to 1e-3 Pa at an open gap (its relaxed pressure decays instead of being set to zero); the reader's rule "pressure above 0 or gap at or below 0 means touching" would call the open gap closed. The analysis takes a pressure above the 10 kPa floor of the contact check as touching.

## 2. Fields at every output time

**Yes.** OFFBEAT writes `D`, `sigma`, `sigmaCyl`, `epsTh`, `gapWidth`, `interfaceP` and `T` (and the rest) at every `writeInterval`, in `times/<t>/`. `solver/run-feed.sh` copies only `T` for a transient (`cp "$d/T" "$OUT/times/$d/"`) and the mechanics fields only from the last time; the probe's `run-case.sh` copies `T D sigma sigmaCyl gapWidth interfaceP epsTh` of every time. For M4 the script would add `D sigmaCyl gapWidth interfaceP` (the reader takes `sigmaCyl` when it is there) to that loop, when `feed-transient.json` and a mechanics marker are present. **Size**: 17.4 KB per written time for these four plus `T` on 68 cells (T 2.6, D 5.0, sigmaCyl 6.4, gapWidth 1.7, interfaceP 1.6 KB), about 255 bytes per cell per time; 200 times is 3.5 MB (all seven fields: 5.8 MB). The case file allows 200 outputs and the work cap `MAX_TRANSIENT_WORK` is 3e6 cell-steps: 2000 steps on 1500 cells with 200 outputs is 76 MB, 3000 cells with 1000 steps (the cap again) and 200 outputs 0.15 GB: D8's cut of the record to the series plus chosen times is needed for large meshes, not for the example. The patch values `gapWidth` and `interfaceP` of `fuelOuter` are single numbers in the patch (the reader's `patchScalar` reads them).

**Where the misses are, and why** (`fails.cjs`):

- **Stress at the first 6 to 9 ms (4 to 9 ms; 4 to 12 ms for 40 micron):** every stress is below 0.13 MPa (0.35 MPa for 40 micron), the bound is 3 % of that plus 10 kPa, and OFFBEAT's own fields carry a noise of 20 to 170 kPa (the steady note found 50 kPa in the axial stress). The check cannot pass there for a reason that is not the physics. The same for the 7 times of the no-contact case.
- **Equilibrium of OFFBEAT's own radial stress (5 times for 20 micron at 18 to 22 ms, 8 times for 40 micron at 18 to 25 ms, 23 times at 13 to 37 ms in the case that never touches):** the cladding's temperature has a front that crosses its 8 cells (the heat enters through the gap and has travelled 0.3 mm by 20 ms); the radial balance by the trapezoid rule over a cell is out by 1.6 to 2.25 times its 2 % + 10 kPa bound there (the first time with contact is among them). After the front has passed it passes at every time to the end of the cooldown; the zero-gap case, where the cladding is heated from the first step, never fails it.
- **Displacement of the explicit contact at the converging factor 0.1:** the overlap of the penalty (2.5 to 2.9 micron of 170) moves the cladding's displacement by 1.8 %, over the 1 % bound at 111 of 117 times, although the pressure is within 3 %. This is the steady sweep's remark ("the displacement missed its 1 % by the micron of overlap") met again: with a thin shell as the soft part, the displacement bound is stricter than the pressure bound. The implicit contact at 0.3 to 0.7 has 0.6 to 0.26 % of overlap and passes.
- **Finite-volume temperatures:** in the pulse (0 to 100 ms) every check passes at every time with contact (the run `r1-g20` has no failure with contact up to 100 ms); in the cooldown OFFBEAT's temperatures differ from FEED's by 1.1 to 1.75 K (0.92 K at most without mechanics), and the checks fail at 20 to 43 s: the stress of a cooling cladding is a few MPa and 1 K gives 4.7 MPa (E alpha dT / (1 - nu) of the steel; 0.66 MPa for the fuel), the cladding's displacement is a few microns and 1 K moves it by 0.3 micron, and the pressure near the reopening changes by about 40 kPa per kelvin (a pressure of 0.3 MPa is then out by 12 to 18 %). **This is the allowance that the plan's verification section already names** ("the stress tolerance carries that term, computed per time from the temperature bound of the thermal check"); the probe adds that the displacement and the contact pressure need it too, and gives its size: 1.75 K here against the thermal check's own bound.
- **Temperatures changed by the mechanics: see question 4.**

**The thermal strain of the independent solution must be piecewise constant per cell.** With the cell temperatures interpolated linearly (and extrapolated to the faces), the same OFFBEAT output fails much more: for 20 micron at 0.7, 49 displacement, 34 stress and 23 contact failures over all times, a worst pressure error of 22 % (14 contact failures with contact), for 40 micron 38 %, and for the zero gap 92 displacement failures; the pressure near the reopening, where the overlap is a fraction of a micron, is the most sensitive (a surface temperature of 1 K is 0.4 micron). OFFBEAT's cell-centred thermal strain is the cell value, and so is the finite-volume solution's. `analyze.cjs --interp` makes the other treatment; M3 should take the cell value (or refine the finite volumes until the choice does not matter).

## 4. Does the mechanics change the temperatures?

**Yes, by up to 1.1 K, with or without a contact.** The same case with the mechanics off (`mechanicsSolver fromLatestTime`, 2.4 outer iterations per step) against with it on, largest cell difference over the 200 written times ([results/comparisons.txt](transient-mechanics-probe/results/comparisons.txt)): 20 micron 1.13 K, 40 micron 1.11 K, zero gap 1.15 K, **no contact (300 micron, wall kept) 1.00 K**. Through the pulse: 0.05 K at 30 ms (0.009 % of the 488 K rise), 0.45 K at 60 ms (0.06 %), 0.77 K at 100 ms (0.095 % of 804 K); in the cooldown 0.87 K at 1.1 s, 1.0 K at 10 s (0.14 %), 0.84 K at 30 s (0.29 % of the 289 K left). This is the steady case's 0.13 %, now in time. It is not the contact (a case that never touches shows it) and the cause was not found (a displaced boundary in the thermal gap, or the order of the thermal and mechanical solves). It matters because FEED's thermal check against the finite-volume solution has a bound sized for the thermal-only run: the mechanics run's OFFBEAT temperatures differ from FEED's finite-volume ones by up to 1.75 K (the thermal-only run by 0.92 K, 0.61 K at the pulse's end), so the thermal check, which stays what it is, has to carry the mechanical addition if it is run on a mechanics result.

## 5. D3: a law of temperature over an 800 K swing

**(b) works, and is the clean answer.** The pinned OFFBEAT's `constantMaterial` picks its elasticity and expansion models by name from the material dictionary (`YoungModulusModel`, `thermalExpansionModel`, read with `lookupOrDefault("...", "constant")`), and two of the models it ships take their coefficients from the dictionary and are polynomials, which is exactly what the library's laws are. No model takes a table.

- **Young's modulus**, `YoungModulusModel steel1515TiTobbe;` with `YoungModulus { par1 63.4208; par2 0.0339266; }`: E = (par1 - par2 T[degC]) x 1e9 Pa, a linear law in T (a printed warning outside 293 to 1273 K, no stop). With `par1` = the modulus at 0 degC and `par2` the slope, it is the library's `a + b T` exactly. Other linear forms: `D9Hofman` (E = (par1 - par2 T[degC]) x 1e6) and `Mo` (fixed coefficients).
- **Thermal expansion**, `thermalExpansionModel steel1515TiGehr;` with `thermalExpansion { par1 -9.425e-5; par2 4.52e-6; par3 9.625e-9; }` for the library's fuel: the thermal STRAIN from 20 degC as par1 + par2 T[degC] + par3 T[degC]^2 (zero at or below 293 K; the stress-free temperature is 20 degC, the dictionary's `Tref` is read and not used by the model; values under 1e-7 are set to 0). The integral of the library's `alpha = a + b T` from 293.15 K is such a quadratic exactly. Other models: Snead SiC (a cubic), Matpro zircaloy, RELAP UO2, Swindeman Hastelloy N, Molybdenum (`(par1 + par2 T)(T - 273.15)`), the PARFUME ones; none takes a table. The model names are those of steels and metals for historical reasons; the keywords are generic.
- **The run**: `l2-g20-p07` (20 micron, implicit 0.7, E and strain by those two models for the fuel, the steel constant, cooldown to 100 s): 2000 + 1998 steps, every step converged, mean 39 / 17.5 outer iterations, **CPU 121 s against 80 s for the constants (+52 %)**. Against the independent solution with the **exact laws cell by cell** (the probe-local finite-element solution `vp.cjs`, checked against `thermoElastic.ts` for constants: the contact pressure to 2e-6, stresses to 0.2 MPa of 100): the pressure error is -0.17 % at closing, -0.26 % at the peak (38.3 MPa), -0.25 % at the end of the pulse, -1.6 % just before the reopening (0.07 MPa); the gap after the reopening is 1.36 micron in both; displacement, stress and contact pass at all times with contact (102), and 6 early stress and 8 equilibrium misses (the closing is later, so the cladding's front lasts longer). The closing is at 25 ms against 22 ms with the 100 ms constants: at 400 K the law's thermal strain is 6.3e-4, the constant's 1.35e-3.
- **(c), re-writing the constants at a restart, also works, to the printed precision.** (i) A stop at 60 ms and a restart with the same constants equals the continuous run: identical before the restart, and after it the largest difference is 3e-11 m in displacement, 300 Pa in stress, 136 Pa in contact pressure, 1e-4 K in temperature (`r1-g20` against `r0b-g20`); (ii) a restart that changes the fuel's constants (E 62.7 GPa and alpha 1.0e-5 per K before 60 ms, 35.53 GPa and 1.2626e-5 after): the two runs differ by 30 micron and 8 MPa of pressure before the restart and, after it, by 1.8e-9 m, 0.21 MPa in stress (0.015 % of the largest), 3.6 kPa in pressure, 0.06 K in temperature against the run that had the new constants throughout; the independent solution with the new constants agrees at 61 ms to -0.27 % (the pressure jumps from 28.7 to 36.9 MPa across the restart). **The elastic state has no memory of the old constants** (the contact's relaxed pressure is forgotten within one step). (A first try, `r2-g20`, was a no-op: both pairs were reduced from a finite-volume run that ends at 60 ms.)
- **The size of D3(a), one pair of constants** (`q5-reduction-error.cjs`; 20 micron; the exact laws against constants, both from the finite-volume temperatures of the time, [json](transient-mechanics-probe/q5-reduction-error.json)): constants reduced at the **same** time as the solution (the best a constant can do) give the contact pressure to 0.0 to 0.4 % but the fuel stress off by 8 % (30 ms) to 41 % (1 s) of the stress scale, 34 to 29 % at 10 to 20 s and 24 % at 30 s, because the modulus varies by 10 to 15 GPa across the fuel in a gradient. Constants reduced **at 30 ms** (mid-rise) are right at 30 ms (2.5 MPa of 32) and then: fuel stress off by 25 % (40 ms), 28 % (60 ms), 41 to 42 % (100 ms and 1 s), 12 % (30 s); **contact pressure 12 to 38 % low from 40 ms to 10 s**, then 8 % high at 15 s and 52 % high at 20 s; the cladding's hoop stress off by 250 to 390 MPa from 40 ms to 3 s (the pressure error times 36). Constants reduced **at 60 ms** (the peak temperature): the pressure right from 60 ms to 100 ms (0.0 to 0.1 %), 5 % high at 1 s, 12 % at 3 s, 24 % at 6 s, 44 % at 10 s, 85 % at 15 s and 180 % at 20 s, the fuel stress off by 12 to 43 %. The two other gaps are the same in kind (`q5-reduction-error.json`). Printing "the approximation" of (a) would print differences of 25 to 100 % and more of the result; (a) is not a usable default for a pulse. (b) gives the laws exactly at +52 % CPU, for any pair that is a linear E(T) and a linear alpha(T) (the library's forms); a table-based law (the cladding in M1, a strength) is not covered and would use (c) windows or a fit.

## 6. Inertia

**The displacement equation of OFFBEAT's `smallStrain` has an inertial term, `fvm::d2dt2(rho, D)`, and FEED's `fvSchemes` switches it off (`d2dt2Schemes { default steadyState; }`)**: the plan's "no inertia keyword" is not exactly right; there is a keyword, in `fvSchemes`, and the scheme is the writer's choice. With the scheme set to `Euler` and the real densities (fuel 5996 kg/m3 and cladding 7900 kg/m3, **unverified**: the figures the example's own text quotes from the K-State SAR Table 13.3; the writer's own `rho` of 10000 is a carrier for the heat capacity; the heat capacity was rescaled so that rho c is unchanged), the run converges (900 of 900 steps, 51 outer iterations on average, the same cost) and gives the static answer: 45 ms, 20 micron, 0.7, same constants: the largest difference over the 45 written times is 5e-11 m in displacement, 2.6 kPa in stress, 16 Pa in pressure, 1e-5 K (`dyn2-g20` against `sta2-g20`; two static runs of the same case that differ only in their end time and the tail of the power table differ by 4e-10 m, a difference not explained and larger than that). This does not prove that the term acts (an inertial force of rho x (1.7e-4 m / (10 ms)^2) = 1e4 N/m3 against an elastic force of 1e10 N/m3 is 1e-6); it shows that switching it on changes nothing here and costs nothing.

The wave transit time across the fuel's wall (`q6-inertia.cjs`): with the modulus of the library and the density above, the bar speed is 3235 m/s at 293 K and 2432 m/s at the pulse's hot end (E extrapolated to 35.5 GPa): **the transit time across the 14.99 mm wall is 4.6 to 6.2 micro-seconds** (3.9 to 5.2 for the dilatational wave), the cladding's 0.1 micro-second. The pulse: 11.5 ms from 10 % of the peak power to the peak, and the fastest change in the table is a doubling in 2.2 ms (0.5 ms table interval). **The doubling time is 360 transit times and the rise 1870**: the plan's "about a hundred transit times" criterion is met 3.6 times over by the fastest segment of the example. (Figures marked unverified remain so until M1 reads the SAR.)

Mechanics makes the run **55 to 140 times** as costly as the thermal one, and the contact is not what costs (a pulse that never touches costs as much): the iterations go to the displacement's relative criterion on a step whose increment is tiny (57 outer iterations per step at 0 to 22 ms, 42 at 22 to 60 ms, 10 at 60 to 100 ms for 20 micron). The cooldown (1998 steps of 50 ms) takes 31 to 85 CPU seconds, one cooldown step of the zero-gap 0.3 case 542 iterations, one of the explicit 0.1 case 712. Against the validator's cap (3e6 cell-steps = 30 to 50 minutes of mechanics at 0.5 ms per cell-step) a mechanics case would need its own cap (the example is 136 thousand cell-steps).

## What it changes

**D7 (the penalty factor).** The explicit contact has no usable factor for the TRIGA pin (0.3 and 0.7 diverge, 0.1 converges but misses the displacement bound). The **implicit contact** (`implicitGapContact`, OFFBEAT's, with `penaltyFactorFriction`) converges at 0.3 to 0.7 and the pressure is within 0.25 to 0.7 % at 0.7 to 0.3 (1/factor); the transient tolerance for the contact pressure can stay the steady 3 % + 10 kPa with OFFBEAT's temperatures, and the factor recommended for the editor is 0.7 or above (not tried above 0.7). Not tested: the implicit contact on the steady sweep of the soft pair, which the steady plan left at the edge of its tolerance; the gain argument says it should be inside by a wide margin (-0.28 % at 0.7 here against -2.7 to -3.1 % with the explicit contact there). Tolerances for the transient are **not** loosened: they are the steady ones, with the additions below.

**D3 (a law of temperature).** Recommend **(b) as the default**: write the library's E(T) and alpha(T) as OFFBEAT's own models (`steel1515TiTobbe`, `steel1515TiGehr`), the independent solution evaluated with the laws cell by cell. (a) is 25 to 100 % off and cannot be printed as a small approximation; (c) works if a law is a table. This changes M3: `thermoElastic.ts` takes constants per solid and the closed form needs them; a solid with E(r) and a thermal strain (r) needs a numerical solution (the probe's finite-element version in `vp.cjs` agrees with the closed form to 2e-6 in the pressure and 0.2 MPa in stress at 300 elements and could be rewritten in `src/` with a refinement test), and the closed form stays as the test of the numerical one. The two models set rules the case has to respect: the stress-free temperature of the expansion model is 20 degC (a case with another reference temperature needs its par1 shifted, and the model returns 0 below 293 K), the modulus law must be linear in T, the strain quadratic.

**D2 (quasi-static).** Holds with a large margin (360 transit times at the fastest segment; the inertial term, if switched on, changes nothing measurable). Keep the warning with the numbers; the density is the open item for M1 (5996 and 7900 kg/m3 come from a figure the example quotes).

**The writer** (all four are needed for a transient with a contact; nothing is changed by M0):

1. `implicitGapContact` with `penaltyFactorFriction` (frictionless, as `frictionCoefficient 0` already says) in place of `gapContact` for a transient with a contact.
2. **`fuelInner` of `0/D` traction-free** (`tractionDisplacement` with zero traction and zero pressure, as at `cladOuter`) for a fuel with a hole. **This is also a defect of the steady mechanics that is already in the product:** the writer writes `zeroGradient` there, uniform heating of the annulus then gives a radial stress of -18.9 MPa at the hole (the exact answer is 0) and a displacement of the hole's cell twice the exact one (1.99 times at 20 ms; 1.05 times at the outer surface), and no test or example runs a mechanics case with a hole (`rg fuelInnerRadius tests/mechanics*` finds none; the mechanics probe lists "an annular fuel" as not probed). A steady mechanics case with a hole would fail its own stress check on the real solver. Not run in the steady case here (the transient run at uniform temperature shows it); the dispatcher should decide whether it is a bug to fix before plan 17.
3. The reader's "touching": a pressure above the 10 kPa floor, not "above 0" (OFFBEAT's `interfaceP` is 1e-6 to 1e-3 Pa at an open gap).
4. The convergence of the displacement in a transient: the criterion `relD 1e-6` is relative to the displacement increment of the step, which is 1e-12 m in the first milliseconds; at a cap of 100 outer iterations exactly those 6 steps (3.6 to 13.4 ms) are not converged, at 1000 none is (151 at most). Either keep 1000 (cost above) or restate the criterion as a time-step criterion in the plan and say it (not done here, and not loosened). The run script collects `D sigmaCyl gapWidth interfaceP` at every time (question 2).

**M3's yardstick** needs three stated additions to the steady tolerances: (i) the thermal strain per cell (above); (ii) a **noise floor** for the stress and equilibrium checks of OFFBEAT's own field (20 to 170 kPa seen; the bound's 10 kPa floor fails at stresses under 0.35 MPa, and the radial balance fails by up to 2.0 times at the cladding's thermal front); (iii) a **temperature allowance** for the displacement and the contact pressure as well as the stress, sized from the thermal bound plus the mechanics' own addition to the temperature (up to 1.1 K here). Without (iii) the finite-volume comparison fails 3 to 4 contact checks in the cooldown, near the reopening.

## Not probed, and caveats

Run-to-run: OFFBEAT's output has 8 significant digits, which is the floor of the restart and inertia comparisons (1e-11 m, 100 Pa).
