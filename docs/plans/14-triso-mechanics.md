## The question and what it changes in the stated scope

FEED states its scope as: thermal results, and for a pin a **thermo-elastic, steady, small-strain** mechanics result; **no irradiation creep, swelling or fission-gas release** (README, [docs/mechanics.md](../mechanics.md), [plan 06](06-mechanics-and-irradiation.md) steps 3 to 6 "later", plan 13 D1). Plan 13 built the particle **thermal only and steady**: a particle case with a `mechanics` section, a history or a gas inventory is refused today (`E_UNSUPPORTED`, [docs/triso.md](../triso.md)). A particle's mechanics is different from the pin's in one way that decides the plan: **what loads a coated particle is mostly irradiation** (the pyrolytic carbon shrinks and creeps, the fission gas and CO build up pressure), not temperature. The probe shows the size: for the benchmark shell stack (IPyC 40 um, SiC 35 um) 25 MPa of gas pressure puts **+104 MPa** of tangential stress in the SiC, and with a PyC shrinkage of 1.5 % added to the same pressure the SiC is at **-846 MPa** (case 4a; the sign is compression). An elastic particle with pressure and temperature but no dimensional change of the carbon would therefore give a number that is wrong in sign and size for an irradiated particle. So the scope change comes in steps, and each step is a different kind of result:

| Stage | Result | What changes in the stated scope |
|---|---|---|
| **E (elastic)** | Stress and displacement of the coating layers from a stated gas pressure, an outside pressure, the thermal strain from FEED's own temperatures, and **free strains the case states** (the carbon's shrinkage as an input with its source, not a model). Steady. | The particle stops being "thermal only" (`assumptions.thermalOnly: false`, as the pin's mechanics case). "No swelling" stays true: dimensional change is an **input**, never computed. README and docs/triso.md need two sentences; plan 13's D1 is marked decided. |
| **I (irradiation)** | The same, with the carbon's swelling and creep computed from a **fast fluence and temperature history**, the buffer's shrinkage opening the buffer-IPyC gap (which then feeds the thermal gap conductance), the gas pressure from a stated gas inventory. A history, not a steady state. | "Steady only" and "no irradiation creep, swelling or fission-gas release" become false **for the particle**. Plan 06's steps 3 to 5 apply to it; the T1 refusal of a transient particle is lifted for this section; new set types (swelling, creep) and a history input appear. |
| **F (failure fraction)** | A probability that the SiC fails, from a strength distribution **the case states with its source**, for one particle and for a sampled population. | FEED gives a probability of failure for the first time. The rule of the temperature limits applies: FEED has no strength of its own; a number without a source is refused. |

It does **not** add, in any stage: kernel migration, IPyC cracking or debonding models, fission-product transport through the layers (OFFBEAT has a diffusion solver; out), a three-dimensional or aspherical particle, SCIANTIX or any fission-gas release model of FEED's own (see D6), a CO production model (a stated amount, or a correlation that has been checked against its source, later), contact between the buffer and the IPyC.

## What the probe found (M0)

Full numbers: [the findings](../spike/triso-mechanics-probe.md) and the run log. In short:

- **OFFBEAT runs every shipped TRISO case without a failure**, 0.2 to 28 s each (the tutorial 18 s; a direct run exits 139 after writing a complete result, as before).
- **Elastic cases against a closed form of my own** (concentric Lame spheres): cell-centre stresses within **0.002 to 0.03 % (tangential) and 0.03 to 0.16 % (radial)** of the layer's peak, for pressure loading (cases 1 to 3), a swelling layer (4a) and a heated shell with thermal expansion (0.02 % tangential, 0.5 % radial, the radial part of the size of the wedge mesh's 0.03 % flux factor, **not separately tested**). So an independent second method for the elastic particle exists, is small and agrees closely.
- **Constant-coefficient creep** (4b, 4c) against an independent time march of my own (the IPyC cut into thin sublayers, Heun's method on the fluence): the SiC stress agrees with the benchmark's expected value to 0.001 % (4b) and 0.1 % (4c). So a second method for creep exists as soon as the creep law and coefficient are stated.
- **The shipped scripts' own errors (0.6 to 3 %) are mostly where the stress is read**: a face value extrapolated from the first cell, or the first cell 2.5 um inside. FEED must read cell-centre values and compare at the cell radius.
- **For the coupled cases 9 to 13 there is no independent solution, and the shipped reference values are not reproduced by this build** (5 to 27 % apart); 5 of the 14 results fall outside the "range of other codes" quoted in the same files; those ranges are wide (case 11: SiC stress from -159 to +439 MPa). These cases cannot establish that a code is right to a few percent.
- **A trap found on the way:** a layered-sphere solution with an anisotropic free strain (creep, anisotropic swelling) that leaves out the `r ln r` term violates equilibrium and gives a SiC stress 35 % wrong; an equilibrium check catches it.
- The thin-shell cases 4d to 7 reproduce their previous values to 0.1 to 0.7 %; the cases with the incremental-updated solver and the gas model (8 to 13) differ from them by 5 to 27 %.

## The failure fraction

**Definition.** FEED does not have a strength of its own (same rule as the temperature limits). A case states, per failure mode, the strength distribution with its source. For the SiC pressure-vessel mode the usual form is a two-parameter weakest-link (Weibull) statistic: `P_f = 1 - exp[-(V_eff/V_0) (sigma/sigma_0)^m]` over the tensile part of the tangential stress, with the Weibull modulus `m` and the characteristic strength `sigma_0` (at a stated reference volume `V_0`) as inputs **(from memory of the PARFUME literature, unverified: PARFUME treats the SiC this way, with a particle failing when its SiC does, and takes IPyC cracking as a separate, coupled mode; no numerical value is given here because I cannot verify one)**. For a population the fraction of failed particles is the mean of `P_f` over the sampled particles.

**Why a sampled population matters, and a warning.** Failure fractions of interest are small (1e-4 to 1e-6 **(unverified order of magnitude)**). Counting failures in a sample has a relative noise of about 1/sqrt(N P_f), so resolving 1e-5 by counting needs of the order of 1e7 particles. Averaging `P_f` over a sample is far less noisy and is what the plan proposes; a tail-sampling method can come later. With the elastic closed form (a millisecond per particle) a sample of 1e5 particles is feasible in FEED's worker; with OFFBEAT's coupled run (8 to 28 s per particle, case runs of M0) 1000 particles are 2 to 8 hours serial, so OFFBEAT is the check on a **representative subset**, as in plan 13 T6. Plan 13 T6's sampler (distributions of kernel radius, each thickness, a power factor, a matrix temperature; a seed) is reused; the failure fraction needs the pressure and the irradiation inputs sampled too, which is stage I's data.

**What it would verify:** the Weibull integral against a quadrature and against the closed form for a uniform stress (a number written in the test), the population mean against the mean of `P_f` of the mean particle plus the Jensen correction by quadrature, the sampler's reproducibility (plan 13 T6). **What it cannot verify:** that the probability is the probability of a real particle failing. That needs measured failure data (irradiation test results), which this project does not have. The result must say so on its face.

## Verification (written before the code)

Stage E, tolerances fixed in the test file before the first run of M3 (proposals from the probe's numbers, to be tightened or loosened with a reason, not after a miss):
- **The layered sphere against the closed form** (a TypeScript port of the probe's solver, tested first against the textbook single shell and a two-layer split of one material, to 1e-7 Pa as in the probe): cell-centre tangential stress within 1 % of the layer's peak plus 1e4 Pa (probe: 0.002 to 0.03 %), radial within 1 % of the peak (probe: 0.03 to 0.16 %, 0.5 % when the temperature carries the wedge's flux factor), displacement within 0.5 %.
- **Equilibrium** of OFFBEAT's own stress, `d(sigma_r)/dr + 2(sigma_r - sigma_t)/r = 0` between neighbouring cells, and of the independent solution (the probe's trap: drop the `r ln r` term and the test must fail).
- **Interface:** the radial stress and the displacement are continuous across each layer interface in OFFBEAT's result within the same tolerances.
- **Thin-wall sanity:** `sigma_t ~ p r / (2 t)` for a thin SiC shell, within the known thick-wall correction (about 5 % for 35 um on 350 um).
- **Sign and symmetry:** yy = zz in the result; compression and tension signs checked on a pure-swelling case and a pure-pressure case.
- **Negative tests (each must fail a check):** layers in the wrong order, a swapped modulus, a stress read at a face instead of a cell centre, a pressure applied outside instead of inside, the anisotropic free strain with its sublayer term removed.
- **Thermal part:** the hollow-sphere closed form and the thin-sublayer solution agree with each other to the discretisation and with OFFBEAT's result; the shell's temperatures are checked against FEED's thermal solution.
- **Regression:** the thermal particle, the pin and the plate are byte-identical with the new section absent.

Stage I adds: the independent time march (from the probe) against the benchmark's expected values for 4b and 4c to 0.2 %, convergence of the march in the fluence step and in the number of sublayers (the probe's Richardson sequence as the model), conservation of the free strain bookkeeping, OFFBEAT against the march on cases fed the **same sourced correlation**, the buffer gap width against the geometry (gap = IPyC inner radius - buffer outer radius, from OFFBEAT's displacements), the ideal-gas pressure against a hand calculation, the time-step rule (no step across a table end), and the stage-F checks above.

## Recommendation

**Do a reduced, elastic version first (stage E), then decide about irradiation and a failure fraction.** Reasons:
1. The elastic layered sphere is the one part where FEED can keep its rule of **two independent methods**: the probe shows OFFBEAT and a small closed-form solver of my own agree to 0.03 % (tangential), so the check is cheap and strong, and there is no contact problem to tune (the thing that made the pin's mechanics expensive). It also teaches the real mechanism, the competition between the gas pressure (tension in the SiC) and the carbon's shrinkage (compression), if the shrinkage is an input.
2. Everything past it rests on **data FEED does not have and must not take from OFFBEAT's files** (swelling and creep correlations, gas inventory, strength distributions), on a history input and on a failure statistic that cannot be validated here. A literature check (M1) is the cheap way to learn whether the data can be had under usable terms, before any coding, and the gate at the end of stage E gives the owner a concrete result to react to.
3. What is **not** recommended now: stage F (a failure probability that nothing here can validate invites over-reading) and any use of OFFBEAT's coupled benchmark values as an oracle (not reproduced by this build, wide quoted ranges). "Do not do it at all" is a reasonable answer if the owner wants FEED to stay a thermal and thermo-elastic tool: the thermal particle of plan 13 already says how hot each layer is, and stage E adds a number that is easy to over-read as "will it fail". If stage E is done, its banner and docs say plainly that an elastic particle with a stated shrinkage is a teaching result.

**Findings and deviations.**
1. **OFFBEAT's wedge puts an error in the tangential strain** of about half the square of the half angle times the total strain: at the thermal particle's 1 degree, 5e-7 on a thermal strain of 3.4e-3, which is a 0.4 MPa offset in the SiC (a thermal stress of 3 MPa) and invisible when pressure or large free strains drive the stress. Found because the thermal case disagreed in a way no tolerance of the plan covered. The mechanics mesh therefore uses a half angle of 0.25 degree (sixteen times smaller) and the stress tolerance has a stated allowance proportional to the square of the angle and the largest strain. The thermal particle's wedge is unchanged.
2. **A free strain is applied through OFFBEAT's polynomial swelling model** at one unit of fluence in one step (the first coefficient is the strain); it is a way to give a number, not a use of OFFBEAT's swelling physics. The strain OFFBEAT applied is read back (`epsilonSwelling`) and compared with the case's (`mechanicsFreeStrain`), and a missing field fails the check.
3. **The mechanics run does not compute the kernel's temperature** (the coating shells only, with the closed-form temperatures): temperature limits are refused with mechanics, and the thermal run of the same particle gives the OFFBEAT temperatures.
4. **The first definition of the equilibrium check** (residual times the cell spacing, the pin's) was too weak: a negative test that removed the SiC's tangential stress altogether passed it. Changed, before any golden was kept, to the residual times the layer's thickness; the tolerance (2 %) is unchanged and the real runs pass with margin.
5. **The optional fluence table of the plan's section was not built** (a free strain is a stated number): optional in the brief, and a table needs a fluence unit and an interpolation rule the plan does not state.
6. **The benchmark's case 1 (a SiC shell alone)** has no analogue: a particle always has the carbon beside the buffer. The SiC alone is in the layered-sphere tests.
7. `thermalSolver fromLatestTime` is OFFBEAT's deprecated name for `constant`; the writer uses `constant`.
