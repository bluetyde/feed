# Plan 17: The stress of a pin through a transient (the TRIGA pulse first): thermo-elastic mechanics against time

Status: drafted 2026-10-20 on the owner's word ("TRIGA pin pulse, draft plan 17"), after the question whether FEED can plot stress against time (it cannot: [mechanics](../mechanics.md) is steady only, and refuses a transient with `E_UNSUPPORTED`). **Not approved; nothing is built.** No source file, test, schema or other plan was changed. Things that come from memory and not from a file I read or a run I made are marked **(unverified)**. The owner's four open questions of the parity draft are answered here as far as this plan needs them: the fuel is the TRIGA pin (decision D1), and the claim is an independent check, not "the same models as OFFBEAT" (the rule of every FEED result).

## What it adds, and what it does not

**Adds:** a pin case may ask for `mechanics` **and** a `transient`. The result then has, at every output time of the run, the displacement and the stress of the fuel and the cladding (radial, hoop, axial, von Mises), the gap between the solids or the contact pressure, and the time series that the editor plots: the largest hoop stress of the fuel and of the cladding, the contact pressure, the gap width, each against time with its maximum and the time of it, and the margin against a stress limit where the case states one and the limit's table reaches the temperature. Every output time is checked against an **independent thermo-elastic solution** at that time's temperatures, as the steady result is ([mechanics.md](../mechanics.md), the four checks).

**Is elastic and quasi-static.** An elastic solid has no memory: the state at a time follows from the temperatures, the pressures and the contact at that time, with no dependence on the path (no friction, no plasticity, no creep: all absent). That is what makes an independent check possible and cheap: the steady solution, evaluated at each output time's temperature field. **The inertia of the solids is not modelled** (OFFBEAT's `smallStrain` is static). For a TRIGA pulse this is sound by a wide margin: a stress wave crosses the 18 mm fuel in about 6 microseconds **(unverified: it takes a sound speed of about 3 km/s, from a modulus near 50 GPa and a density near 6000 kg/m3 for U-ZrH1.6; FEED holds the modulus and no density)** against a pulse that rises over milliseconds (FWHM 11.4 ms in the example). A transient faster than about a hundred transit times is refused or warned (decision D2).

**Does not add:** creep, plasticity, swelling, densification, cracking, relocation, fission gas, hydrogen **kinetics** (see D4), friction, a failure criterion, a transient with a burnup or with hot dimensions (as today), a stack or a plate or a particle with a transient mechanics (a particle's mechanics stays steady: [plan 14](14-triso-mechanics.md)), a strain-rate dependence of any property. **A stress here is not a verdict**; nothing is compared with a strength unless the case states a limit. A TRIGA's real pulse limits are **temperature** limits (the SAR's two fuel limits, which the case already checks); stress against time is a teaching and trending result.

## The pulse it is built around

[`examples/ut-triga-pulse-4p40-limits.json`](../../examples/ut-triga-pulse-4p40-limits.json) (read, not run for this plan): a TRIGA $4.40 pulse, **illustrative** (the SAR gives the peak power and the peak temperature but not the curve; the curve is an assumed sech^2): 100 ms in steps of 0.05 ms (2000 steps), 122 points of power history, 40 outputs, a peak of 237.6 MW/m (an 11.4 ms FWHM), the fuel 18.161 mm radius with a 3.175 mm central hole, the cladding 0.508 mm thick, 60 + 8 radial cells, the gap a conductance of 4096 W/m2/K **with a cold gap width of zero** (the fuel radius equals the cladding's inner radius), the film 10 kW/m2/K at 20 degC, the interior adiabatic over a pulse this short, **824 degC (1097 K) at the peak by construction**. The library's fuel (`triga-uzrh-h16`, sourced): modulus linear in temperature from 62.7 GPa at 20 C to 41.4 GPa at 650 C (**valid 293 to 923 K only**), Poisson 0.32, expansion linear in temperature (valid to 1073 K), and the cladding (`ss304-k166`): 190 GPa constant (a secondary source, **no range**), expansion 17.2e-6 per K constant (a secondary source, no range). **Two facts follow that the plan has to face:** the pulse's peak (1097 K) is **outside** the fuel's modulus range and its expansion range (the existing rule reduces a law to a constant at the area-mean temperature of the closed-form profile and warns outside the range: for a pulse the temperatures change by 800 K during the run, so a constant reduced at one time is wrong at another: see "A law of temperature in a transient" below); and a TRIGA cladding usually runs **below 400 C**, where FEED holds **no strength** (the SS304 tables of `library/strengths.json` are 400 to 1100 C ultimate and 400 to 870 C yield, read by eye from a figure, about 10 %): the stress curve will exist, the verdict against a limit for the cold cladding will not, unless M1 finds a source.

## A law of temperature in a transient

OFFBEAT's `constant` material takes one Young's modulus and one expansion coefficient per solid: today a law is reduced to constants on the closed-form profile of the steady case, at the area-mean temperature ([mechanics.md](../mechanics.md), "A law of temperature becomes a constant"). In a pulse the temperature of the fuel moves by 800 K, and a constant is wrong at most times. Options (decision D3): (a) reduce at the **time of the largest stress** or at a stated reference time, said in the result; (b) write OFFBEAT's own temperature-dependent forms if the pinned build has a model that takes a table (to be found by M0; OFFBEAT's survey found none for U-ZrH and none for 304 by name); (c) advance by **re-writing the constants** per time window (a restart: the elastic state has no memory, so a restart at a time loses nothing, which is the one thing elasticity gives for free). Recommendation: M0 looks for (b) and measures (c); if neither is clean, **(a) with the independent solution built with the same constants and a stated, time-dependent difference from the exact solution computed from the laws** (the independent solution **can** use E(T) and alpha(T) exactly cell by cell: its own difference from the constant-reduced one is a number FEED can print as the size of the approximation, as the steady case prints a warning outside the range).

## What a result may claim

"Thermo-elastic stress of a pin through a transient, quasi-static, small strain, a frictionless contact, constants reduced from the laws as stated. **Not a failure assessment**: no creep, plasticity, swelling, cracking, gas release or hydrogen kinetics; no strength is compared unless the case states a limit; a stress in a hydride fuel is **not** the quantity that limits a TRIGA pulse (the SAR's fuel temperature limits do)." Every result, export and plot carries that sentence. A result outside any property's stated range is labelled out of domain, as the temperatures are.

## Decisions (to confirm or change)

- **D1. Scope.** The **pin** only, a TRIGA pulse first (decided by the owner, 2026-10-20: "TRIGA pin pulse"). Not a stack, a plate, a particle, a compact or a pebble (each keeps its refusal; a stack with mechanics stays refused). Any pin that today accepts mechanics and a transient separately gets this once it passes the gate; the LWR pin's cladding creep is not here.
- **D2. The quasi-static assumption.** A transient whose fastest power change is within about a hundred wave transit times of the fuel radius is refused (or only warned: to be decided with M1's density source); the transit time needs a density, which FEED's cases do not carry: an `elastic wave check` would need `density` in the mechanical sets, or a conservative stated bound (unverified numbers above). Recommendation: a **warning** with the number (the time a wave needs, the shortest time the power changes by a factor of two) and no refusal, until a density is in the library.
- **D3. A law of temperature in a transient.** As above; recommendation: M0 decides between (b), (c) and (a); (a) is the fallback with a printed approximation.
- **D4. Internal gas pressure in a pulse.** (i) a constant, stated; (ii) a table against time, stated; (iii) the hydrogen **equilibrium** pressure of the U-ZrH at each time's temperature (the closed-form or finite-volume peak or mean fuel temperature), **labelled an equilibrium assumption, not a kinetics model**: in 10 ms the hydrogen cannot redistribute, so the equilibrium pressure at the instantaneous temperature is an upper-bound style figure (to be read against Simnad's own statement: M1). The table is built from FEED's independent temperatures **before** the run (open loop) and checked afterwards against OFFBEAT's own temperatures (a mismatch beyond a stated bound fails a check). Recommendation: all three, with (iii) the TRIGA default and the label.
- **D5. Stress limits where a table does not reach.** Today a cell outside the limit's table is not judged and **the check fails** (nothing is extrapolated, and a limit that cannot be judged does not pass). For a transient the cold cladding is outside the SS304 tables for part of the run or all of it. Recommendation: the check is **judged where the table covers**, reports the fraction of times and cells not judged, is **never failed by coverage alone**, and **fails** if a judged point is above the strength; when nothing is judged the verdict is `na` with the reason. Steady cases keep their rule.
- **D6. The strength of the cladding below 400 C and the temperature dependence of its modulus.** Needs a **sourced** table (M1): without it the TRIGA pulse's cladding stress has no limit to be read against. Candidates to look for: the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code Section II Part D tables for type 304 and the standard annealed minimum properties **(unverified: from memory only; read the source before any number enters the library)**; the NUREG or SAR chapters on TRIGA cladding that give a temperature-dependent modulus. If no source is found with usable terms, the plan's M5 shows the stress and no verdict and says why.
- **D7. The contact penalty factor for the soft fuel against steel.** The steady sweep found it the known limit: a factor of 0.7 is needed for the contact to converge, the pressure is then 2.7 to 3.1 % low, at the edge of the 3 % tolerance, and the stress check can fail by about 1 MPa ([mechanics.md](../mechanics.md)). A pulse closes and opens the gap during the run: the contact state changes with time. M0 measures how OFFBEAT behaves through the closing (convergence at each step, the penalty factor, the pressure against the independent solution); the plan's tolerances for the transient are fixed after M0 from that sweep and **not loosened later**.
- **D8. Output volume.** The fields at every output time (40 in the example, 68 cells, 3 stress components and the displacement: small). A run with 1000 outputs and 10000 cells is bounded by the validator's existing transient work cap; the results record is cut to the series plus the fields at a chosen set of times (the time of the maximum, the start, the end) if the size matters. Decided at M4.
- **D9. Scope wording.** README line 11 and [mechanics.md](../mechanics.md) say "steady cases only" and "not combined with a transient": the sentences that change are listed at the end of the plan; the owner confirms them before M2.

## Verification (written before the code; tolerances fixed before the first run and not loosened afterwards)

The independent solution is `src/core/thermoElastic.ts` evaluated per output time, with the independent finite-volume temperatures of the same time (`transientFv.ts`), the contact and the gas pressure of that time. **The temperatures OFFBEAT used differ from the independent ones by the existing thermal bound** (a few tenths of a kelvin in a pulse), and a stress is sensitive to a temperature difference by about `E alpha dT / (1 - nu)` (about 1 MPa per kelvin of difference across a solid at 45 GPa, 1.5e-5 per K and a Poisson ratio of 0.32, so 0.3 K gives about 0.3 MPa **(unverified: the arithmetic from the library's constants at the pulse's hot end)**): the stress tolerance carries that term, computed per time from the temperature bound of the thermal check, as the closed-form checks carry an allowance elsewhere. The steady tolerances are the base: displacement 1 % of the largest displacement of the solid + 1e-9 m, stress 3 % of the solid's stress scale + 10 kPa, equilibrium 2 % + 10 kPa, contact pressure 3 % + 10 kPa, a gap 2 % + 1e-8 m.
- **Closed forms with time in them (hand-written in the tests, no shared code):** (a) both solids heated uniformly and equally by a ramp, free: no stress, displacement alpha dT r at every time (the cladding by its own coefficient); (b) the fuel heated uniformly against a cladding at fixed temperature: the interference pressure of the two-cylinder Lame solution at each time, from the closed-form gap closure time onward and zero before it; (c) the parabolic temperature profile of a heating fuel (a source in a cylinder, the Timoshenko hollow-cylinder solution in time) with the cladding free; (d) the gap **re-opening** on cooling: the contact pressure falling to zero at the closed-form time and the gap width rising after it.
- **No memory:** the stress at a time equals the stress of the same solid at the same temperatures computed with no history: a test runs the independent solution at one time directly and through a march of earlier times and the two agree to 1e-12 (the property that justifies the check).
- **The real solver:** the pulse example's analogue with the stated mechanics, the loss of cooling, a cooldown after the pulse (the gap reopens), each with every check passing at every output time on OFFBEAT's real output; a fixed seed of cases for the contact (the sweep of the steady plan over three gaps, three stiffness ratios and the penalty factors, at three times each) fixes the transient tolerance for the soft pair (D7).
- **Negative tests (each must fail a check):** the output of time k read as time k + 1; the displacement of another solid; the stress read at a face instead of a cell centre; the gas pressure applied for the wrong time; a pressure table shifted by one output; the temperature of the independent solution one output late; the modulus constant reduced at a wrong time (D3, with the printed approximation larger than the tolerance).
- **Regression:** a steady mechanics case, a transient without mechanics, a stack, a plate and a particle are byte-identical to today with the new combination absent (the same fixture technique as plan 14's `writtenCasesUnchanged`).
- **The quasi-static warning (D2):** a case whose fastest power change is within the stated number of transit times gets the warning with the numbers; a slow case gets none.
