# Teaching cases

Each case carries a `label` (synthetic or sourced), units on every number, and in its `citation`/`description` what is read from where. Sourced cases are checked against the source's own numbers in `tests/`.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `synthetic-pin.json` | Illustrative pin with closed-form answers (the oracle pin). Synthetic. |
| `annular-synthetic-pin.json` | The same with a 1 mm central hole (insulated). Synthetic. |
| `lwr-uo2-burnup.json` | A typical PWR pin at 20 kW/m with UO2 fuel at 40 MWd/kgHM (FRAPCON-3.4's modified NFI conductivity) and Zircaloy cladding (MATPRO's conductivity): the two laws are sourced, everything else is chosen. A frozen-burnup snapshot. Synthetic. |
| `synthetic-pin-hot.json` | The oracle pin with hot dimensions on (free linear expansion from 20 °C, typical coefficients that are not sourced). Synthetic. |
| `ksu-triga-hot-element.json` | K-State TRIGA hottest element, 1.25 MW, prescribed wall temperature; reproduces SAR Table 4.8. Sourced. |
| `ksu-triga-1p25mw-film.json` | The same with the SAR's film coefficient (eq. 7); reproduces eq. 9. The SAR's eq. 7 and Table 4.8 disagree by 8 K; see the case. Sourced. |
| `ut-triga-hot-channel-trace.json`, `ut-triga-hot-channel-annular.json` | UT-Austin hot channel against TRACE's digitized profile (Figure 4.30), solid and with the zirconium rod. Comparison cases, not oracles. Sourced, with values read from a figure. |
| `synthetic-pulse.json` | A 10 ms triangular power pulse (50 kJ/m) on the synthetic pin from a cold start; the fuel interior reaches the adiabatic value E/c. Synthetic. |
| `synthetic-pulse-operating.json` | The same pulse on top of a steady 20 kW/m: the run starts from the pin's steady operating state. Synthetic. |
| `synthetic-pulse-kinetics.json` | A $3 reactivity insertion on the synthetic pin: the power history is GENERATED by six-group point kinetics with fuel-temperature feedback (peak 168 MW, about 11 ms wide, 265 K), not typed in. Synthetic constants except Keepin's textbook delayed-neutron set. |
| `ut-triga-pulse-4p40.json` | A TRIGA $4.40 pulse built from UT-Austin SAR numbers (peak power, 824 C peak, heat capacity law). The SAR gives no power curve; the sech^2 shape is ASSUMED. Illustrative. |
| `ksu-triga-loss-of-pool.json` | The K-State hottest element at 1.25 MW (film case), then a scram and the pool lost at 10 s: the film coefficient falls to 100 W/m2/K, decay heat from an ASSUMED curve; a boundary history. Synthetic, illustrative. |
| `synthetic-coastdown.json` | The synthetic pin at 20 kW/m with a pump trip, a scram and a warming coolant: the film follows an ASSUMED flow^0.8 decay; a boundary history. Synthetic, illustrative. |
| `ksu-triga-cosine-stack.json` | The K-State TRIGA element (film case, SAR limits) as **a stack of 10 slices** over the 38.1 cm fuel length (the length is the UT-Austin SAR's Table 4.1, which FEED's UT cases already use): an ASSUMED cosine power shape with the average set so the continuous peak is the SAR's 62.021 kW/m, and a pool that warms an ASSUMED 5 K from 20 C at the bottom to 25 C at the top. Real solver: hottest slice 6 of 10, 776.81 K (503.7 C), 646 K under the 1150 C fuel limit; the one-dimensional closed form of that slice is 4.4 K higher and the end slices are 11 K hotter than theirs. Properties and limits sourced as in the K-State cases; shape, warming and average are assumptions. Synthetic, illustrative. |
| `synthetic-plate-loss-of-cooling.json` | **The F17 question**: how fast does a plate heat up when its cooling is lost? A plate stack (10 slices over 0.381 m, cosine, 100 kW per metre of height and width on average; the synthetic pin's numbers as a slab) from its steady state, the film coefficient falling from 30000 to 100 W/m2/K at 2 s (an ASSUMED round number) with the power left on for 60 s. Real solver: the middle of the plate goes from 448 K to 925 K (+477 K, 8.7 K/s over the last 48 s and still rising), the end slices from 324 K to 405 K. No boiling, critical heat flux, melting, scram or decay heat. Synthetic, illustrative. |
| `synthetic-triso-compact-steady.json` | A generic coated particle (250/100/40/35/35 um, OpenMC Studio's illustrative dimensions) in a compact of 10 W/cm3 at 35 % packing fraction (11.65 mW a particle), in a 900 K, 2000 W/m2/K matrix (ASSUMED). Conductivities are FEED's synthetic numbers (no sourced PyC, SiC or UO2 set is in the library). Real solver: kernel centre 905.09 K, 5.1 K above the matrix, 2.1 K of it across the buffer. Synthetic, illustrative; no claim about failure. |
| `synthetic-triso-compact-pulse.json` | The same particle from its steady state with a 10 ms triangular overpower to 0.6 W (about 51 times the steady power), synthetic heat capacities, five illustrative limits of 1000 K (one per region, each says it is chosen for the example). Real solver: the kernel peaks at 929.5 K at 20 ms (+24.4 K), the smallest limit margin is 69.7 K (kernel), and at 0.5 s the particle is 0.9 K above its start. Synthetic, illustrative; no claim about failure. |
| `synthetic-triso-population.json` | **A sampled population of the generic particle**: 2000 particles (seed 20261016) drawn from ASSUMED normal spreads of a few percent in the kernel radius and each layer's thickness, a lognormal power factor (sigma of ln x 0.1) and a normal matrix temperature (900 K, 15 K), at 1000 MW/m3 in the kernel and a 900 K, 2000 W/m2/K matrix, on **48 cells per region**. Run population (the editor, or `POST /api/population`) gives a kernel peak of 929.0 K on average (sd 15.5 K, 5 % 903.6, 95 % 953.6, 99 % 965.6 K, from 867.6 to 978.4 K); the case's own particle is 928.66 K. Five of its particles (the hottest, median, coldest, thickest buffer, thinnest SiC) were run through the real solver with every check passing (`tests/golden/offbeat-triso-pop/`). A sample of a stated population, not a prediction for a real core. Synthetic, illustrative. |

The four newest cases (`ksu-triga-cosine-stack`, `synthetic-plate-loss-of-cooling`, `synthetic-triso-compact-steady`, `synthetic-triso-compact-pulse`; plan 12 A9 and plan 13 T7) were run through the real solver with every check passing; the solver's output is kept in `tests/golden/examples/` and read again, without the solver, by `tests/exampleGolden.test.ts` (made by `tests/exampleRuns.test.ts` with `FEED_SOLVER=1 FEED_SAVE_GOLDEN=1`). Editing one of these cases without re-running the solver fails that test. Their numbers are in [docs/plans/12-axial-stack-and-plate.md](../docs/plans/12-axial-stack-and-plate.md) (A9 record) and [docs/plans/13-triso-particle.md](../docs/plans/13-triso-particle.md) (T7 record).
